# Flood, storm surge and landslide exposure

Tago sits on the Pacific seaboard of Surigao del Sur. Flood, storm surge, landslide and river flooding are not
theoretical concerns here, and knowing which of them affects **your barangay** is the difference between a
general worry and a decision.

**This page cannot tell you that yet**, and this is what stands between it and being able to.

## What is published, and what is not

**The municipality has not published a hazard assessment.** Checked on 9 August 2026 against its website and
against the Citizen's Charter.

**The national hazard service does publish one** — HazardHunterPH, run by the national geohazard programme,
returns an assessment for flood, storm surge, landslide and other hazards. It is the right source, and this
project intends to cite it directly rather than re-report it.

## Why it is not cited here yet

**The national service answers a point on a map, not a place by name.** It takes coordinates and returns the
hazards at that spot.

To use it for Tago per barangay, this project needs two things it does not have:

1. **The list of Tago's barangays.** Not published on the municipality's site. The national statistics
   authority publishes it, and on the date above its site could not be read by this project — the page is
   public and permits automated reading, but the host returns an automated-traffic challenge.
2. **Coordinates for each barangay.** Same source, same obstacle.

Both are recorded as dated gaps in this project's register, and both are the kind of thing one person with a
browser can close in an afternoon.

## What this page will not do in the meantime

- **It will not estimate.** "Coastal barangays are exposed to storm surge" is a reasonable sentence and a
  useless one — it tells a resident nothing they did not already know, while sounding like an assessment.
- **It will not read the map itself.** Deriving a hazard rating by picking a point near the municipal hall and
  applying it to the whole municipality would be a computation presented as a citation.
- **It will not borrow a neighbouring municipality's assessment.** Exposure is local; that is the entire point
  of a hazard map.

## What you can do now

**Check the national service directly.** [HazardHunterPH](https://hazardhunter.georisk.gov.ph/) is free and
public, and it will give you an assessment for a specific location — including your own address — which is more
precise than anything this page could publish about a barangay as a whole.

For an evacuation centre, see the **Evacuation centres in Tago** page, which records what is known about
those.

This page is re-checked every 90 days, and the date above changes when it is.
